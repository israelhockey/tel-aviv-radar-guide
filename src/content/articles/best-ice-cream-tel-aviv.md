Ice cream season in Tel Aviv lasts about nine months, and on a humid August night the queues outside the best gelaterias can stretch down the block. People here eat ice cream after dinner, on the way to the beach, at midnight with friends: it's a social ritual as much as a dessert.

The scene is a mix of much-loved Israeli chains, serious Italian-style gelato shops and small places experimenting with local flavours: halva, tahini, date honey (silan), pistachio, malabi (rosewater milk pudding) and fruit sorbets made from whatever's in season. Most shops let you taste before you choose, so ask.

## The picks

[[pick: Anita | "La mamma del gelato" started in Neve Tzedek and became a Tel Aviv obsession, with branches around the city. The original on Shabazi Street in Neve Tzedek is the one to visit on a summer evening.]]

## Flavours to try

- **Halva:** sweet sesame, often with chunks of real halva. Very Israeli.
- **Tahini or sesame:** nutty, savoury-sweet and excellent with date honey.
- **Pistachio:** the benchmark for any serious gelato shop.
- **Malabi:** rosewater milk pudding, sometimes topped with syrup and nuts.
- **Seasonal sorbets:** mango, watermelon and pomegranate when they're in season, all vegan.

## FAQ

### What is the most famous ice cream in Tel Aviv?

Anita, which started in Neve Tzedek, is probably the best known, along with the Israeli chain Golda. Gelato fans also swear by Otello and Riva Reno.

### Where can I get ice cream late at night?

Riva Reno on Nahalat Binyamin stays open until two in the morning on Thursdays and Fridays. Many gelaterias are open until midnight in summer.

### Is there vegan ice cream in Tel Aviv?

Yes. Fruit sorbets are usually vegan, and most gelaterias offer several dairy-free flavours, often made with coconut or almond milk. Ask to see which ones.

### Is ice cream in Tel Aviv kosher?

Some shops and chains have kosher certification and some don't. Kosher shops close for Shabbat. Check each place page for details.
