Tel Aviv has a sushi habit. Israelis embraced it in the 1990s and never looked back, and today there are more sushi bars per street than almost anywhere outside Japan, from delivery chains to serious omakase counters. Rolls here often get a local twist: sweet potato tempura, cream cheese, spicy mayo and plenty of avocado.

But the city's Asian food scene goes well beyond sushi. There's a celebrated pan-Asian restaurant that's often named among the best in Israel, proper ramen bars, Thai kitchens, Vietnamese spring rolls, and, in south Tel Aviv, Chinese restaurants that serve the city's large community of migrant workers.

Each pick links to its own page with today's hours, a map and what's nearby.

## The picks

[[pick: Taizu | One of Tel Aviv's most acclaimed restaurants: a stylish, buzzing room on Menachem Begin Road serving creative dishes inspired by the street food of Asia, from India to China and Southeast Asia. Book ahead, order lots of plates to share, and save room for dessert. A top choice for a special night out.]]

[[pick: Men Tenten | A ramen and izakaya bar on Nahalat Binyamin that stays open until one most nights and until three on Thursdays. Rich bowls of ramen, small plates and Japanese drinks. The best late-night bowl in the city.]]

[[pick: Koko Neko | A ramen spot in Florentin with tables outside, popular with the neighbourhood crowd. A great bowl of noodles before a night in Florentin's bars.]]

[[pick: The Green Roll: Vegan Sushi Bar | Proof that sushi doesn't need fish. The Green Roll on Ahad Ha'am Street makes creative fully vegan rolls that convert sceptics, in a small, friendly space.]]

[[pick: Thai 148 | Thai food at Dizengoff 148, open from noon until late every day. Curries, noodles and stir-fries for a relaxed dinner on Dizengoff.]]

[[pick: Minna Tomei | An Asian restaurant on HaArbaa Street near Sarona, open every day from noon to midnight. A lively option for dinner near the Sarona complex.]]

[[pick: Kansai | A kosher sushi restaurant on Yigal Alon Street, open during the week and reopening after Shabbat on Saturday night. One of the better kosher sushi options in the city.]]

[[pick: Chop Chop | A casual Asian café and sushi bar on Ibn Gvirol with tables outside and long hours. Handy for a quick lunch or an easy dinner.]]

[[pick: Kanu Spring Rolls | Spring rolls and Asian street food on Herzl Street, open until midnight most nights. Cheap, cheerful and very good.]]

[[pick: Xing Long | A Chinese restaurant on Shlomo Street in south Tel Aviv. The area around the central bus station is home to many migrant workers from Asia and Africa, and its small restaurants serve some of the most authentic food in the city.]]

[[pick: Samui | Thai food in Florentin, a neighbourhood favourite for a relaxed dinner.]]

## FAQ

### What is the best Asian restaurant in Tel Aviv?

Taizu is the most celebrated, for creative dishes inspired by Asian street food. Book ahead.

### Where can I get ramen in Tel Aviv?

Men Tenten on Nahalat Binyamin, open late, and Koko Neko in Florentin are two of the best.

### Is there vegan sushi in Tel Aviv?

Yes: The Green Roll is an entirely vegan sushi bar, and most sushi restaurants have several vegetable rolls.

### Where can I find kosher sushi?

Kansai is a kosher sushi restaurant, and several sushi chains have kosher branches. Kosher restaurants close for Shabbat.

### Where can I find authentic Chinese food in Tel Aviv?

South Tel Aviv, around the central bus station and Neve Sha'anan, has small Chinese and Asian restaurants serving the local migrant community.
