Markets ("shuk" in Hebrew) are where Tel Aviv shops, eats and gossips. Each has its own character.

## Carmel Market (Shuk HaCarmel)

The big one, running from Magen David Square towards the sea. Fruit and vegetables, spices, cheese, cheap clothes, and an ever-growing number of small food stalls and bars. Open Sunday to Friday; Friday mornings are packed and wonderful, and everything shuts by mid-afternoon before Shabbat.

Next door, the **Nahalat Binyamin crafts fair** fills the pedestrian street on Tuesdays and Fridays. Our [Carmel Market food walk](/walks/carmel-market-food-walk) strings it all together.

## Levinsky Market

Less a market than a few streets in Florentin of old spice shops, Balkan and Persian delis, dried fruit and nuts, and some of the city's best-loved cafés. Great for a slow browse and a coffee. See our [Florentin guide](/magazine/florentin-guide).

## Jaffa Flea Market (Shuk HaPishpeshim)

Antiques, vintage furniture, oddities and junk in the lanes south of the clock tower, surrounded by restaurants and bars that come alive at night. Friday mornings are best for treasure hunting. Part of our [Old Jaffa walk](/walks/old-jaffa).

## Sarona Market

An indoor food hall in the Sarona complex, with delis, bakeries, international food counters and wine bars. More polished and pricier than the open-air markets, and open on Saturdays too.

## Hatikva Market

In the south-east of the city, a traditional neighbourhood market with lower prices and a local crowd, plus old-school grill restaurants.

## The Port market

At the Tel Aviv Port, a covered market of food producers, plus a farmers' market. Combine it with a walk along the deck and the Yarkon.

## Tips for market days

- Go early on Friday for the buzz, or late in the day for the best prices.
- Bring cash and a bag. Most stalls take cards now, but not all.
- Taste before you buy: vendors are happy to let you try.
