Shabbat, the Jewish day of rest, runs from sunset on Friday to nightfall on Saturday. Tel Aviv is Israel's most secular city, so plenty stays open, but the rhythm changes and public transport mostly stops. This week's exact times are on our [Shabbat page](/shabbat).

## Friday: the best day of the week

Friday morning is when the city is at its liveliest: markets are packed, cafés overflow and everyone is out before the shops close in the afternoon. Many bars run a "Friday noon" party scene. Shops and markets close a few hours before sunset.

## What's open on Shabbat

- **Restaurants, cafés and bars:** many stay open, particularly in the centre, Jaffa and the Port. Kosher restaurants close.
- **The beach:** open as always, and at its busiest on Saturday.
- **Shops:** most close, but some areas have shops open, including the Port, Sarona and Jaffa Port. Convenience stores and some supermarket branches are open too.
- **Museums:** several major museums open on Saturday.

## Getting around

- **Trains and the light rail** don't run.
- **Most buses** stop, but a few private and intercity lines run.
- **The city's free weekend shuttle,** "Naim BaSofash", runs several lines across Tel Aviv on Friday evening and Saturday. Live on our [map](/live?layers=shabbat).
- **Taxis and ride apps** work as normal, with a weekend surcharge on the meter.
- **Tel-O-Fun bikes and scooters** are available all weekend.

## Etiquette

Most of Tel Aviv is relaxed about Shabbat. In religious neighbourhoods, and in Jerusalem, avoid driving through closed streets, smoking or using your phone conspicuously in synagogues and religious areas.

## Saturday night

When Shabbat ends, the city switches back on: shops reopen in some areas, buses and trains restart, and Saturday night is a big night out.

[[tip: Have plans for Saturday morning: a long brunch, the beach, or a walk in Yarkon Park are how locals do it.]]
