Tel Aviv is small: you can cycle from Jaffa to the Yarkon in half an hour. But each neighbourhood is a different life. Here's where to look depending on what you want, with the trade-offs nobody puts in the listing. Each name links to a full neighbourhood page with live construction data, places to eat and what's on nearby.

## For families

**[Old North](/area/old-north)** is the classic choice: quiet, tree-lined streets, good schools and kindergartens, the Yarkon Park and the beach both close. The catch is construction: it's one of the busiest areas in the city for building renewal, so check the specific street.

**[New North](/area/new-north)** and **[Bavli](/area/bavli)** give you more space for the money, newer buildings, more parking and the Yarkon Park on the doorstep. Fewer bars and a slower pace, which is the point.

**[Ramat Aviv](/area/ramat-aviv)** is green and suburban, next to Tel Aviv University and two big museums, with good schools. Further from the beach-and-cafés centre.

## For a first apartment and nightlife

**[Florentin](/area/florentin)** is the city's young, loud south: street art, bars on every corner, small flats in old blocks. Great if you want to walk out of the door into a scene; hard if you need sleep before midnight.

**[City Centre](/area/city-centre)** around Dizengoff and Ben Yehuda puts the beach, cafés and nightlife within a few minutes' walk. Expect old buildings, TAMA 38 scaffolding and very difficult parking.

**[Lev HaIr](/area/lev-hair)** around Rothschild and Sheinkin is the postcard: Bauhaus, boulevards, kiosks. Central and walkable, and priced like it.

## For character and the sea

**[Jaffa](/area/jaffa)** gives you the sea, stone alleys, markets and some of the city's best food, with a mixed Arab and Jewish community and a pace of its own. **[Ajami](/area/ajami)** next door is quieter, with sunsets over the water.

**[Neve Tzedek](/area/neve-tzedek)** is the prettiest neighbourhood in the city, low houses and quiet lanes a few minutes from the beach, and one of the most expensive.

**[Kerem HaTeimanim](/area/kerem-hateimanim)** sits between the Carmel Market and the sea. Lively, central, and noisy from early morning on market days.

## For value

**[Yad Eliyahu](/area/yad-eliyahu)** in the east has bigger flats for the money, quick access to the highway and the business district, and its own market.

**[Shapira](/area/shapira)** and **[Neve Sha'anan](/area/neve-sha-anan)** are the most affordable areas close to the centre, diverse and changing fast. Visit at different times of day before deciding.

## How to choose between two flats

1. **Walk the street at night.** Bars and buses change everything after dark.
2. **Check the construction.** Type each address into our [construction checker](/check) to see permits under review, approved projects, building sites and night works within 100 to 500 metres.
3. **Compare them side by side.** Our [apartment comparison](/compare) puts up to three addresses next to each other with a noise score for each.
4. **Think about the next two years, not just today.** A quiet street with five approved permits will not be quiet next year.

Read our [renting guide](/magazine/renting-an-apartment-tel-aviv) for what to check at a viewing and what the lease must say, and our [TAMA 38 explainer](/magazine/tama-38-explained) to understand the scaffolding.

## FAQ

### What's the best neighbourhood in Tel Aviv for families?

The Old North, New North, Bavli and Ramat Aviv are the most popular with families, for their schools, parks and quieter streets. Check construction on the specific street, because the north is also where much of the building renewal is happening.

### Where do young people live in Tel Aviv?

Florentin, the City Centre and Lev HaIr are the most popular with students and young professionals, for the nightlife, cafés and walkability.

### What's the cheapest area near the centre?

Shapira, Neve Sha'anan and parts of Jaffa and Yad Eliyahu usually have the lowest rents close to the centre. Visit at different times of day before you commit.
