Café culture is the heartbeat of Tel Aviv. People meet, work, flirt and break up over coffee here, and whole neighbourhoods are organised around their cafés. Sit down anywhere along Rothschild, Dizengoff or in Florentin on a weekday morning and you'll see laptops, dogs under tables, and the city's business being done one cappuccino at a time.

The local order is the "hafuch" (literally "upside down"), a milky coffee somewhere between a cappuccino and a latte. Old-timers still drink "botz" ("mud"), Turkish-style coffee made by pouring hot water over finely ground beans, and "nes", instant coffee, is a nostalgic classic. Over the past decade a serious specialty scene has grown up too, with roasters sourcing single origins and baristas who'll happily talk you through a pour-over.

## How to order coffee in Tel Aviv

- **Hafuch:** the local cappuccino-latte. Ask for "hafuch katan" (small) or "gadol" (large).
- **Hafuch al soya / shibolet shu'al / shkedim:** with soy, oat or almond milk.
- **Espresso kaful:** a double espresso.
- **Botz:** strong Turkish-style coffee, often with cardamom (hel).
- **Ice coffee:** in summer, "ice café" is often a frozen, sweet slushie. If you want coffee over ice, ask for "kafe kar" or an iced Americano.

## FAQ

### What is a hafuch?

Israel's favourite coffee: espresso with plenty of steamed milk and foam, somewhere between a cappuccino and a latte. The name means "upside down".

### Where is the best specialty coffee in Tel Aviv?

Cafelix in Florentin is the best-known specialty roaster, and Nahat is a favourite with coffee lovers. Florentin has the highest concentration of good specialty cafés.

### What time do cafés open in Tel Aviv?

Many open at seven, some earlier. Neighbourhood cafés often close in the late afternoon, and many kosher cafés are closed on Saturday.

### Are cafés open on Saturday?

Most non-kosher cafés are open on Saturday and busy with brunch. Kosher cafés close from Friday afternoon until Saturday night.

### Can I work from cafés in Tel Aviv?

Yes, it's very common, and most cafés have free Wi-Fi. Busy brunch spots may prefer you don't camp at weekends.
