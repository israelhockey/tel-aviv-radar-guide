Tipping in Israel is expected in some places and unusual in others. Here's the short version.

## Restaurants and cafés

Leave **10 to 15 percent** for table service; 12 percent is about standard, more if the service was great. Service is usually **not** included, but check the bill: some places, especially for groups, add it automatically.

When you pay by card, tell the server how much tip to add, or leave the tip in cash. Servers often rely on tips for a large part of their income.

## Bars

For table service, tip as in a restaurant. At the bar, rounding up or a few shekels a drink is fine.

## Counter service

No tip is expected when you order at a counter: falafel stands, takeaway, bakeries. A tip jar is a nice option, not an obligation.

## Taxis

Tipping taxi drivers isn't expected. Make sure the meter is on, and round up if you like.

## Tours and guides

For a guided tour, tipping the guide is customary if you enjoyed it. A driver on a private tour usually gets a smaller tip too.

## Hotels

Not required, but a small tip for porters and housekeeping is appreciated.
