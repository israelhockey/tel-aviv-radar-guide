If you only eat in restaurants in Tel Aviv, you're missing half the city. The best meals here often come wrapped in a pita and cost a fraction of a sit-down dinner. They're eaten standing at a counter, with tahini running down your wrist, and they're the food Israelis get nostalgic about when they live abroad.

Street food here is a story of immigration. Falafel came with Middle Eastern and North African communities, sabich with Iraqi Jews, burekas with Turkish and Balkan Jews, shawarma from across the Levant. Tel Aviv took all of it, added a salad bar's worth of toppings, and turned it into an art form. Then chefs like Eyal Shani turned the humble pita into a restaurant concept that's now exported around the world.

Here's where to go, what to order and how to do it like a local. Every pick links to its own page with hours and a map.

## The picks

[[pick: Miznon | Celebrity chef Eyal Shani's pita kitchen and the place that made the pita fashionable. The menu changes but the classics stay: pitas stuffed with slow-cooked beef, chicken liver or ratatouille, and the famous whole roasted cauliflower, charred and served in paper. Loud, chaotic, brilliant. There are branches in Paris, New York and beyond, but this is home.]]

[[pick: Sabich Frishman | The city's best-known sabich, from the stand that made the name famous near the corner of Frishman and Dizengoff. Fried eggplant, hard-boiled egg, potato, salad, tahini and amba (pickled mango sauce) packed into a pita. There's usually a queue; it moves fast. Vegan version available without the egg.]]

[[pick: HaKosem | "The magician" is the falafel stand everyone recommends, and it lives up to it: crisp green falafel, a generous salad bar and, famously, a free falafel ball handed to you while you wait. Also does excellent shawarma and sabich. Kosher and always busy.]]

[[pick: Frishman Falafel | A classic falafel counter on Frishman Street, a few minutes from the beach. Simple, fresh and cheap: exactly what you want after a swim.]]

[[pick: Itzik HaGadol | Not street food exactly, but the ultimate meat feast and a Jaffa institution. Sit down, and before you've ordered the table fills with a parade of small salads, followed by grilled skewers. Bring friends and an appetite.]]

[[pick: Elias Shawarma | A kosher shawarma spot on Shoncino Street. Slices of spiced meat carved off the spit into a pita or laffa with all the trimmings.]]

[[pick: Mama Borekas | Flaky burekas on Levinsky Street in Florentin, with fillings from cheese and potato to spinach, and vegan options. Get one with a hard-boiled egg, grated tomato and pickles, the classic way.]]

[[pick: HaMosad for Special Sandwiches | Creative sandwiches on Ibn Gvirol, with vegan options and tables outside. A good change from pita.]]

[[pick: the 4 colors falafel | A neighbourhood falafel stand in the Old North, the kind of place locals have been going to for years.]]

[[pick: Ovad's Sabich | Strictly speaking it's in Givatayim, just east of Tel Aviv, but Ovad's sabich has a cult following and many locals insist it's the best in the country. Worth the short trip if you're serious.]]

## What to order

- **Falafel:** deep-fried chickpea balls in a pita with salad, pickles and tahini. Cheap, filling and vegan.
- **Sabich:** fried eggplant, hard-boiled egg, potato, tahini and amba in a pita. Iraqi Jewish breakfast food turned national obsession.
- **Shawarma:** spit-roasted meat (usually turkey, lamb or chicken) carved into a pita or rolled in a laffa flatbread.
- **Burekas:** flaky pastries with savoury fillings. Best fresh from the oven, ideally in the morning.
- **Hummus:** a meal in itself. See [the best hummus](/magazine/best-hummus-tel-aviv).
- **Jachnun and malawach:** Yemenite pastries, mostly found on Saturday mornings and in the Yemenite Quarter.

## How to order like a local

1. Say what you want, in a pita ("be-pita") or a laffa (a big wrap).
2. You'll be asked what you want inside. "Hakol" means "everything".
3. Hot sauce: "harif" is chilli, "schug" is the green Yemenite one. Amba is the tangy mango sauce.
4. At many falafel stands you fill your own salad at the counter. Don't be shy.

## FAQ

### What is the most famous street food in Tel Aviv?

Falafel and sabich, followed closely by shawarma and hummus. Miznon's stuffed pitas have become famous too.

### Where is the best sabich in Tel Aviv?

Sabich Frishman is the best known in the city. Many locals also make the pilgrimage to Ovad's Sabich in neighbouring Givatayim.

### Is street food in Tel Aviv cheap?

Compared to restaurants, yes. Falafel and sabich are some of the cheapest filling meals you can get in the city.

### Is Tel Aviv street food vegan?

A lot of it is. Falafel, hummus and sabich without the egg are all vegan, and most places will happily adjust. See [the best vegan food in Tel Aviv](/magazine/best-vegan-tel-aviv).

### What is amba?

A tangy, slightly spicy pickled mango sauce brought to Israel by Iraqi Jews. It's essential on sabich and great on falafel.
