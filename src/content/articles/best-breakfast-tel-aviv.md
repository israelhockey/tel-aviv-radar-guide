Breakfast might be the meal Tel Aviv does best. The Israeli breakfast was born on the kibbutz, where people worked the fields at dawn and came back starving, and it shows: eggs any style, a chopped salad, cheeses, spreads like labneh and tahini, olives, bread and a pot of jam, all arriving at once and covering the table. Add a cappuccino ("hafuch") and fresh orange juice and you're set until dinner.

Then there's shakshuka, eggs poached in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce and served in the pan, which arrived with North African Jewish immigrants and became a national dish. And, because this is Tel Aviv, there's everything else: American pancakes, French pastries, vegan bowls and eggs Benedict at midnight.

Weekends are brunch time. On Friday and Saturday mornings the popular cafés fill up fast, often with a queue, so go early or book where you can. Each pick below links to its own page with today's hours and a map.

## The picks

[[pick: Dr. Shakshuka | The most famous shakshuka in town, served in a big, rambling courtyard restaurant near the Jaffa clock tower. The house style is North African, rich and spicy, and comes bubbling in the pan with bread for mopping. It's touristy, and it's still worth it. Combine it with a morning in the Jaffa flea market.]]

[[pick: Puaa | A café in the heart of the Jaffa flea market where the mismatched vintage furniture is part of the charm. Relaxed, arty and perfect for a long weekend brunch before browsing the antique shops outside.]]

[[pick: Benedict | Tel Aviv's temple to breakfast: eggs Benedict in many versions, pancakes, shakshuka and full breakfasts served all day long. The Rothschild Boulevard branch is the classic. Expect a wait at weekend peak times.]]

[[pick: Meshek Barzilay | A vegetarian restaurant in Neve Tzedek with a farm-to-table approach and a brunch menu that wins over committed carnivores. Vegan options throughout. Great for a slow weekend brunch.]]

[[pick: NOLA American Bakery | An American bakery and café on Dizengoff for bagels, pancakes, cinnamon buns and proper cookies. Open every day from early morning to the afternoon, with tables outside.]]

[[pick: Kirsh Cafe | A kosher café on Dizengoff with a big breakfast menu and outdoor seating, open from 7am. One of the better options if you need a kosher breakfast in the Old North.]]

[[pick: The Streets | A busy café on King George Street open from early morning to late night every day, with a long menu, vegan options and plenty of space to work or catch up. Useful when you want breakfast at an odd hour.]]

[[pick: Lachmanina | A bakery café on Marmorek Street, known for its breads and pastries. Ideal for a quick, excellent breakfast or a coffee and croissant to go.]]

[[pick: Annabelle | A neighbourhood café on King George Street that opens at a quarter past six, which makes it an early riser's dream. Good coffee, tables outside and a simple breakfast menu.]]

[[pick: Cafe Bayit | A laid-back Florentin café with outdoor tables, where locals linger over long breakfasts. Very much a neighbourhood spot.]]

## What to order

- **The Israeli breakfast ("aruchat boker yisraelit"):** two eggs any style with a spread of salads, cheeses, dips and bread. Often for one person, sometimes for two. It's a lot of food.
- **Shakshuka:** eggs poached in spiced tomato sauce. Variations include green shakshuka (with spinach and herbs) and shakshuka with feta or merguez.
- **Sabich for breakfast:** fried eggplant, egg and tahini in a pita. Technically street food, but a classic morning choice. See [street food](/magazine/best-street-food-tel-aviv).
- **Burekas:** flaky pastries filled with cheese, potato or spinach, often served with a hard-boiled egg and grated tomato.
- **Coffee:** order a "hafuch" for a cappuccino-style coffee. See [the best coffee](/magazine/best-coffee-tel-aviv).

## FAQ

### What is a traditional Israeli breakfast?

Eggs (fried, scrambled or as an omelette) served with a chopped vegetable salad, several cheeses and spreads such as labneh, tahini and cream cheese, olives, bread, butter and jam, plus juice and coffee. It started on the kibbutz and is now a hotel and café staple.

### Where is the best shakshuka in Tel Aviv?

Dr. Shakshuka in Jaffa is the most famous. Many cafés across the city serve excellent versions too, including green shakshuka with spinach and herbs.

### Where can I get breakfast all day in Tel Aviv?

Benedict serves breakfast all day, and The Streets on King George Street is open from early morning until late at night.

### When is brunch in Tel Aviv?

Weekend brunch is on Friday and Saturday, the Israeli weekend. Saturday morning is the busiest time. Many kosher cafés are closed on Saturday.

### Is there kosher breakfast in Tel Aviv?

Yes. Kirsh Cafe on Dizengoff is kosher, and many chain cafés have kosher branches. Kosher places close for Shabbat, from Friday afternoon to Saturday night.
