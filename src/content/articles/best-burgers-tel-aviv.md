Tel Aviv is a burger city. It doesn't look like one at first, with all the hummus and the shakshuka, but walk down Ibn Gvirol or Dizengoff at ten at night and count the queues. Israelis eat a lot of beef, butchers here grind their own blends, buns come from neighbourhood bakeries, and the competition is fierce enough that a mediocre burger joint rarely lasts a year.

What makes a Tel Aviv burger different? Usually it's the toppings and the attitude more than the meat. Expect grilled onions, aioli in a dozen flavours, sometimes a fried egg or a slab of entrecôte fat, and a side of sweet potato fries. Many places cook to order (ask for "medium"), and almost all of them have a vegan patty, because this is also one of the most vegan-friendly cities on earth.

We've picked the places we'd send a friend to, from the counters people cross town for to the ones that save you at 2am. Each pick links to its own page with opening hours, a map and what's nearby.

## The picks

[[pick: Vitrina | The name that comes up first in every argument about Tel Aviv's best burger. Vitrina is a narrow counter on Ibn Gvirol with a few stools and a queue that often spills onto the pavement, and the burger is the reason: a juicy, well-seasoned patty in a soft bun, with a short list of toppings that don't get in the way. Order the house burger the first time, eat it standing up if you have to, and don't be in a rush.]]

[[pick: Benz Brothers | Right across Ibn Gvirol from Vitrina, which makes for an easy two-burger evening if you're serious about research. Benz Brothers made its name with butcher-quality beef and a menu that's a little more adventurous, with specials that rotate. There's a vegan option too, so mixed groups are covered.]]

[[pick: Prozdor | A small, buzzy burger bar on Mendele Street, a few minutes from the beach, open from noon until midnight every day. It's the kind of place that works for a quick lunch or a beer-and-burger evening, and the vegan patty is a real option rather than an afterthought.]]

[[pick: Moses | An American-style restaurant and bar with a big menu, a lively room and burgers at the centre of it all. Moses is a dependable choice for a group that can't agree on anything, and the Sarona branch is kosher, which makes it one of the better kosher burger options in the city.]]

[[pick: Junky Burger | Florentin's late-night smash burger stop on Levinsky Street, open until midnight most nights and later on Thursdays and Saturdays. Thin, crispy-edged patties, plenty of sauce and a vegan version. Perfect before, or after, a night in Florentin's bars.]]

[[pick: 90's Diner | A diner-style burger joint on Yefet Street in Jaffa that keeps late hours, until the small hours on Friday nights. Useful to know about if you're out in Jaffa after the restaurants have closed, and there are vegan options on the menu.]]

[[pick: Port 19 | A kosher burger bar on Shlomo HaMelech Street with tables outside and vegan burgers alongside the beef. Being kosher, it closes on Friday afternoon and stays closed for Shabbat, but during the week it's a solid, relaxed choice in the city centre.]]

[[pick: GDB Burger | Another strong Ibn Gvirol contender, with outdoor seating and long hours (until midnight most days). The menu covers the classics plus vegan patties, and it's a good fallback when the queue at Vitrina is too long.]]

[[pick: Shake Shack | The New York chain's Tel Aviv branch on Dizengoff, open from noon to midnight. If you know Shake Shack from abroad you know what you're getting: smash-style burgers, crinkle fries and shakes. Not the most local choice on this list, but a reliable one.]]

[[pick: Agadir | One of Israel's original burger bars, with a menu built around burgers, beer and late nights. Agadir has been around long enough to feel like an institution, and it's a safe bet when you want a proper sit-down burger and a pint.]]

## How to order like a local

- **Doneness:** many places ask how you'd like it. "Medium" is the default for most people; say "medium rare" if you like it pink.
- **Size:** menus often list patty weights, usually around 200 to 250 grams. A double is a commitment.
- **Extras:** common add-ons include fried egg, grilled onions, mushrooms, cheese, and sometimes a slice of foie gras or entrecôte fat at the fancier places.
- **Sides:** sweet potato fries are standard. Onion rings are a treat.
- **Drinks:** pair it with a local craft beer. See [the best beer bars](/magazine/best-beer-bars-tel-aviv).

## FAQ

### What is the best burger in Tel Aviv?

Ask ten locals and you'll get ten answers, but Vitrina on Ibn Gvirol comes up more than any other. Benz Brothers, across the road, is the usual counter-argument.

### Where can I get a kosher burger in Tel Aviv?

Moses in Sarona and Port 19 on Shlomo HaMelech Street are both kosher. Kosher places close for Shabbat, from Friday afternoon until Saturday night.

### Where can I get a burger late at night?

Florentin and Jaffa are your best bets after midnight. Junky Burger stays open late most nights, and 90's Diner in Jaffa is open into the small hours on Fridays. The live list above shows what's open late tonight.

### Are there good vegan burgers in Tel Aviv?

Yes. Almost every burger place in this guide has a vegan patty, and many are excellent. Tel Aviv is often called one of the most vegan-friendly cities in the world.

### How much does a burger cost in Tel Aviv?

Tel Aviv is expensive. Expect a gourmet burger to cost noticeably more than at a chain, and more again with extras and fries. Business lunch deals at sit-down places are often the best value.
