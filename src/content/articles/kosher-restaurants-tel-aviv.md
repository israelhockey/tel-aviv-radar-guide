Tel Aviv is Israel's most secular city, and many of its best-known restaurants aren't kosher. But that doesn't mean keeping kosher here is hard. There are hundreds of kosher restaurants, cafés, bakeries and street food stands, from old-school family restaurants to steakhouses, sushi bars and burger joints, and the business district around Yigal Alon and Sarona is full of them.

This guide explains how kosher works in practice, lists our favourite kosher places, and links to a live map so you can see what's open right now. Kosher status can change, so always check the certificate on the wall.

## How kosher works in Tel Aviv

- **The certificate.** A kosher restaurant displays its certificate (teudat kashrut) near the entrance, issued by the city's rabbinate or a private supervision body. Ask to see it if you're unsure. Some diners only trust certain supervisors, such as "mehadrin" (a stricter standard).
- **Meat or dairy, never both.** Kosher places serve either meat (bsari) or dairy (chalavi). Dairy places are often cafés, pizzerias and fish restaurants. "Parve" means neither meat nor dairy.
- **Shabbat.** Kosher restaurants close on Friday afternoon, before sundown, and reopen on Saturday night, usually about an hour after Shabbat ends. See [this week's Shabbat times](/shabbat).
- **Chains.** Many Israeli café and fast-food chains have kosher branches alongside non-kosher ones. Check the individual branch.

## Shabbat in Tel Aviv for kosher travellers

If you're observing Shabbat, plan ahead: buy food before Friday afternoon, or arrange meals with your hotel. Many hotels offer Shabbat meals and Shabbat elevators. There are synagogues in every neighbourhood, including the Great Synagogue on Allenby Street. See [Shabbat in Tel Aviv](/magazine/shabbat-in-tel-aviv) and the [Shabbat times page](/shabbat).

## FAQ

### Are there many kosher restaurants in Tel Aviv?

Yes, hundreds, even though Tel Aviv is mostly secular. They're concentrated in the business district around Yigal Alon and Sarona, and spread across the city.

### Are kosher restaurants open on Saturday?

No. Kosher restaurants close on Friday afternoon and reopen on Saturday night, usually about an hour after Shabbat ends.

### What's the difference between kosher and kosher mehadrin?

Mehadrin is a stricter level of supervision. Some observant diners only eat at mehadrin places. The certificate on the wall shows the level.

### Is there kosher street food in Tel Aviv?

Plenty. HaKosem is the most famous kosher falafel and shawarma stand, and many hummus places are kosher too.

### How can I tell if a restaurant is kosher?

Look for the kosher certificate near the entrance, which shows the supervising authority and whether the place is meat or dairy. If you can't see one, ask.
