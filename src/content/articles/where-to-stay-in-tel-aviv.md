Tel Aviv is compact. Almost everywhere central is within a half-hour walk of the sea, so the question isn't really "how close to the action", it's what kind of action you want outside your door. Here's how the main areas compare.

## The beachfront (HaYarkon Street)

The strip of big hotels along HaYarkon and the promenade, roughly from Hilton Beach down to the Dan Panorama area. You wake up to the sea, Gordon and Frishman beaches are across the road, and the promenade is perfect for a morning run.

**Best for:** first-timers, families, anyone who wants the beach above all.
**Keep in mind:** the big seafront hotels are the city's priciest, and the streets right behind them are busier with traffic than charm.

## The Old North

Leafy streets, Bauhaus apartment blocks, Ben Yehuda and Dizengoff full of cafés, and the Yarkon Park and the Port a short walk away. It's where many locals would choose to live.

**Best for:** longer stays, couples, a calmer base that's still central.
**Keep in mind:** fewer big hotels, more boutique places and apartments.

[[stay: Old North Tel Aviv | Boutique hotels and apartments near the Port and Yarkon Park.]]

## Rothschild and the city centre

The White City's heart: Rothschild Boulevard, Habima, Sheinkin and the Bauhaus streets around them. Bars, restaurants and nightlife on every corner, and the light rail's Allenby and Carlebach stations close by.

**Best for:** food and nightlife lovers, architecture fans, short city breaks.
**Keep in mind:** it's about 15 to 20 minutes' walk to the sea, and it's lively until late.

[[stay: Rothschild Boulevard Tel Aviv | Design hotels in the White City.]]

## Neve Tzedek

The city's oldest neighbourhood: low houses, boutiques, galleries and quiet lanes, between the Suzanne Dellal Centre and HaTachana. It feels like a village and it's minutes from the beach.

**Best for:** romantic trips, boutique hotel lovers.
**Keep in mind:** few rooms and high demand, so book early.

[[stay: Neve Tzedek | Small boutique hotels in the city's oldest quarter.]]

## Jaffa

Ancient stone lanes, the old port, the flea market and a different rhythm from Tel Aviv proper. Jaffa has some of the most beautiful hotels in the country, in restored historic buildings.

**Best for:** history, atmosphere, sunsets.
**Keep in mind:** you'll be taking taxis, scooters or a long walk to central Tel Aviv's nightlife.

[[stay: Jaffa | Historic hotels near the old city and the port.]]

## Florentin

Street art, bars, workshops and the Levinsky Market. Florentin is young and gritty, and home to several of the city's best hostels.

**Best for:** backpackers, budgets, night owls.
**Keep in mind:** it's loud on weekends, and not polished.

[[stay: Florentin Tel Aviv | Hostels and budget rooms near the bars.]]

## Practical tips

- **Shabbat matters.** From Friday afternoon to Saturday evening most buses and the light rail stop. A central location makes the weekend much easier. See [what's open on Shabbat](/shabbat).
- **Book ahead for holidays and events.** Pride week, the High Holidays, Passover and big summer weekends fill up fast.
- **Check for a shelter.** Many hotels list their protected room or shelter; it's worth knowing where it is. Our [city guide](/guide) shows public shelters on the map.
