Tel Aviv's pizza scene has grown up fast. Ten years ago "pizza" mostly meant a cheap slice with a squeeze of ketchup-sweet sauce. Today there are slice shops run by people who learned in New York, wood-fired ovens turning out blistered Neapolitan-style pies, and bars where pizza is the reason the crowd shows up. Prices vary a lot, from a quick slice to a sit-down pie with a glass of wine.

A few local quirks: toppings lean Mediterranean (think olives, feta, za'atar, roasted eggplant), corn as a topping is surprisingly common, and the best places often only open in the evening. Florentin has the highest concentration of good pizza per street, and Allenby is where you go when it's 3am and you need a slice.

## The picks

[[pick: Joe's Pizza | New York-style slices on Allenby Street that keep going long after everything else has closed: until three, four or even five in the morning towards the weekend. Big, thin, foldable slices, a short menu and a vegan option. The definition of a Tel Aviv late-night saviour.]]

[[pick: Brooklyn Pizza | A New York-style slice shop on King George Street, right in the centre. Grab a slice to go or a whole pie for a group.]]

[[pick: Teder FM | Part bar, part radio station, part pizza place, on Jaffa Road near HaTachana. Teder is an institution for a reason: a big, loud, friendly courtyard, DJs, cold beer and pizza coming out of the oven until late. Go with friends on a warm evening.]]

[[pick: La Tiger | A Florentin pizza bar that opens in the evening (and from lunchtime at the weekend), with tables outside and a crowd that lingers. Thin, well-charred crusts and good drinks to go with them.]]

[[pick: HaBarbanel Pizza Bar | A neighbourhood pizza bar on Abarbanel Street in Florentin. Relaxed, unfussy and a good stop before heading to the area's bars.]]

[[pick: Giuseppe Pizza | Another Florentin favourite on Vital Street, open into the night and with vegan options for mixed groups. Good for a quick slice while exploring the street art around Vital.]]

[[pick: Pizza Lila | A small Florentin pizzeria on Merhavia Street, open from morning to late most days. Cosy and local, steps from the Levinsky Market.]]

[[pick: GaliRoma | A boutique pizzeria on Dizengoff, open in the evenings and from midday on Fridays. A handy stop on a Dizengoff stroll.]]

[[pick: Italkia be Tahana | A proper Italian restaurant near HaTachana in Neve Tzedek, open every day from noon. Pizza alongside pasta and Italian classics: a good choice for a sit-down dinner after walking the old neighbourhood.]]

[[pick: Tony Vespa | Israel's well-known pizza-by-the-slice chain, with branches around the city. Fast, reliable and often open late. Not artisanal, but you'll be glad it exists.]]

[[pick: Zota Pizza | A kosher pizza and pasta option in the north of the city. Closed on Shabbat, open through the week.]]

## Kosher and vegan pizza

Kosher pizzerias serve dairy-only pizza (no meat toppings) and close for Shabbat, from Friday afternoon to Saturday night. For vegan pizza, many places offer plant-based cheese; Joe's Pizza and Giuseppe both have vegan options.

## FAQ

### What's the best pizza in Tel Aviv?

It depends what you're after. For a New York-style slice, Joe's Pizza and Brooklyn Pizza. For a night out with pizza and beer, Teder FM. For a sit-down Italian dinner, Italkia be Tahana. Florentin has the most good options close together.

### Where can I get pizza at 3am in Tel Aviv?

Joe's Pizza on Allenby Street stays open until three, four or five in the morning depending on the night. Tony Vespa branches often stay open late too.

### Is there kosher pizza in Tel Aviv?

Yes. Kosher pizzerias are common, especially away from the centre; Zota Pizza is one example. Kosher pizza is always dairy, with no meat toppings, and kosher places close for Shabbat.

### Do Tel Aviv pizzerias deliver?

Most do, through the delivery apps used in Israel, which also show what's open now.
