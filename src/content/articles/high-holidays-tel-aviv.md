Every autumn, a run of holidays changes the rhythm of the city for about a month. Here's what each one means for getting around and going out.

## Rosh Hashanah

The Jewish New Year. Families gather for dinner with apples and honey, and the two days of the holiday work like Shabbat: no public transport and most businesses closed.

[[holiday: Rosh Hashana]]

## Yom Kippur

The Day of Atonement is the quietest day of the year. Shops, restaurants, TV and radio stop, there are no cars on the roads, and the city's streets fill with families on bikes and kids on scooters. It's an unforgettable thing to see.

[[holiday: Yom Kippur]]

## Sukkot

A week-long harvest festival. You'll see sukkot (temporary huts) on balconies and outside restaurants, and the city runs family festivals during the holiday week.

[[holiday: Sukkot I]]

[[tip: On the main holiday days, plan around transport: buses and trains stop like on Shabbat. See [Shabbat & holidays](/shabbat).]]
