Nothing starts an argument in Tel Aviv faster than asking where to get the best hummus. Everyone has a place, usually the one near where they grew up, and everyone is sure theirs is the right answer. That's the fun of it.

Real hummus here isn't the cold dip from the supermarket. It's served warm, often just made, smooth and loose enough to swirl, topped with whole chickpeas, olive oil, paprika or cumin, sometimes ful (stewed fava beans) or a hard-boiled egg. You scoop it up with pita and raw onion. A proper hummusiya makes one big batch in the morning, serves until it runs out, and closes. That's why the golden rule is simple: go early.

Below are the places we'd send a friend, with the classics in Jaffa first and the best neighbourhood spots after. Every pick links to its own page with today's hours and a map.

## The legends

[[pick: Abu Hassan | The name everyone mentions, and for once the hype is earned. Abu Hassan (officially Ali Karawan) has been serving hummus in Jaffa for decades, at shared tables where you'll sit elbow to elbow with construction workers, families and tourists. Service is lightning fast and the menu is tiny: hummus, hummus with ful, and msabbaha. The original on Shivtei Israel Street is the one to visit. Get there before lunch; when the pot is empty, the door closes.]]

[[pick: Ali Karawan | The Ajami branch of the Abu Hassan family business, a short walk from the original and often a little calmer. Same style, same silky hummus, same rule about turning up early.]]

[[pick: Abu Maruan | A Jaffa favourite on Yefet Street in Ajami, the kind of no-frills place locals keep to themselves. Warm hummus, generous portions and a quieter room than the big names, which makes it a good choice if you want to linger.]]

[[pick: Hadj Hummus | Another Ajami hummusiya worth knowing, close to the old port. Combine it with a walk through Jaffa: our [Old Jaffa walking tour](/walks/old-jaffa) passes nearby.]]

## The neighbourhood heroes

[[pick: Hummus Ashkara | The answer when the craving hits at a strange hour. Ashkara on Yirmiyahu Street in the north of the city opens at five in the morning and, Sunday to Thursday, doesn't close until two at night. It's a north Tel Aviv institution, and the hummus with whole chickpeas is the order.]]

[[pick: Garger HaZahav | A small, friendly spot on Levinsky Street in Florentin, right in the spice-market streets. Generous bowls, good extras, fair prices, and it's open late into the afternoon, which is not a given with hummus. Pair it with a browse through the Levinsky Market.]]

[[pick: Humus Eliyahoo | A kosher hummusiya on Maon Street in Florentin, open through the afternoon on weekdays and until early afternoon on Fridays. Popular with people who work nearby, and a good option if you keep kosher.]]

[[pick: Halevantini | Hummus, shawarma and Levantine dishes on Ben Yehuda Street in the Old North, open for lunch on weekdays. A handy stop between the beach and Dizengoff when you want something more substantial than a pita.]]

[[pick: Hummus Abu Adham | A small hummusiya with loyal regulars and excellent warm chickpeas. Worth seeking out if you're collecting the city's best bowls.]]

[[pick: HaKosem | Famous above all for its falafel (you'll often be handed a free ball while you wait), HaKosem also does a very good plate of hummus. It's kosher, central and always busy.]]

## How to order like a local

- **Hummus** ("hummus") on its own, or **hummus masbacha** with warm whole chickpeas on top.
- **Msabbaha** is warm chickpeas in tahini, lemon and garlic: richer and chunkier than hummus. Order it if you see it.
- **Ful** is stewed fava beans, often spooned over hummus. Earthy and filling.
- **Hummus with egg:** a hard-boiled egg on top is classic, especially in the morning.
- **The sides:** pita, raw onion wedges, pickles and a hot green or red sauce (schug) usually come free. Use the onion as a spoon.
- **Drinks:** lemonade or a can of soda. Nobody orders wine with hummus.

## FAQ

### Where is the best hummus in Tel Aviv?

Abu Hassan in Jaffa is the most famous, and many locals would say the best. But Tel Aviv's hummus scene is personal: Hummus Ashkara, Garger HaZahav and the Ajami places all have devoted fans. Try two or three and pick your own.

### What time should I go for hummus?

Morning to early afternoon. Traditional hummus places make one batch a day and close when it runs out, often by two or three in the afternoon. Hummus Ashkara is the exception, open from early morning until late at night most days.

### Is hummus vegan?

Plain hummus, msabbaha and ful are naturally vegan: chickpeas, tahini, lemon, garlic and olive oil. Hummus with egg obviously isn't. Tel Aviv is a great city for vegans in general; see [our vegan guide](/magazine/best-vegan-tel-aviv).

### How much does a plate of hummus cost?

Hummus is one of the best-value meals in Tel Aviv. A plate with pita and sides costs a fraction of a restaurant meal, and it's filling enough to be lunch.

### What's the difference between hummus and msabbaha?

Hummus is blended smooth. Msabbaha (also spelled masabacha) is warm whole chickpeas in a tahini-lemon sauce, so it's chunkier and richer. Many places will serve hummus topped with msabbaha.
