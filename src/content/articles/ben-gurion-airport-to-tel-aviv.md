Ben Gurion Airport (TLV) is about 20 kilometres south-east of Tel Aviv. Depending on traffic and the time of day, the city is 15 to 45 minutes away. These are your options.

## By train (the easy way)

The airport has its own railway station beneath Terminal 3. Trains run to Tel Aviv's four central stations: **HaHagana**, **HaShalom** (next to Azrieli), **Savidor Center** (Arlozorov) and **University**. The ride to HaShalom takes around 15 to 20 minutes.

- Follow the signs for the train from the arrivals hall.
- Pay at the station with a Rav-Kav card, a ticket from the machines, or a public transport payment app.
- From the Tel Aviv stations you can switch to the light rail, a bus, a scooter or a taxi.

**Note:** trains don't run on Shabbat, roughly from Friday afternoon until Saturday night, or on major Jewish holidays.

## By taxi

There's an official taxi rank outside arrivals. Taxis use the meter (ask for the "moneh" if the driver doesn't start it). There's a surcharge at night and on Shabbat and holidays.

You can also book a ride through taxi apps such as Gett or Yango, which are widely used in Israel.

## By bus

Buses connect the airport with nearby cities, but for Tel Aviv the train is almost always faster and simpler. Check the Moovit app for the current lines.

## Arriving on Shabbat

On Friday evening and Saturday the train isn't running and most buses aren't either. Your options are a taxi, a ride app, or a pre-booked transfer. Shared minibus taxis ("sherut") also run some routes over the weekend.

[[tip: Download Moovit before you fly. It shows live public transport in Israel, including which lines are running on Shabbat.]]

## Going back to the airport

- Allow plenty of time: Israeli airport security is thorough. Airlines usually advise arriving three hours before an international flight.
- The train is the most predictable option at rush hour; the Ayalon highway can be very slow.
- On Saturday, trains usually start again after Shabbat ends. See [this week's Shabbat times](/shabbat).
