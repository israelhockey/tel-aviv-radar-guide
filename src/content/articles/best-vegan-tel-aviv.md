Tel Aviv is often called the vegan capital of the world, and once you've eaten here you'll understand why. A big share of Israelis say they're vegan or vegetarian, the city has dozens of fully vegan restaurants, and almost every other menu has clearly marked vegan dishes. Waiters won't blink when you ask.

It helps that so much of the local food was plant-based to begin with. Hummus, falafel, tahini, msabbaha, majadra, stuffed vegetables, salads of every kind: you could eat vegan for a week here without setting foot in a vegan restaurant. Add a generation of young chefs cooking creatively with vegetables, and you get a city where meat is genuinely optional.

## Fully vegan

[[pick: Anastasia | A fully vegan café on Frishman Street, open from breakfast to dinner (and through Saturday morning). Big breakfasts, bowls, cakes and excellent coffee with plant milks. A Tel Aviv vegan landmark.]]

[[pick: Goodness | Vegan fast food done properly: burgers, fries and comfort food, all plant-based. The place to take a sceptic who thinks vegan food is all salad.]]

[[pick: BANA | A vegan restaurant on Nachmani Street, open every day from late morning to late evening, with a creative, colourful menu built around vegetables, grains and sauces.]]

[[pick: Zakaim | A vegan restaurant near Rothschild serving home-style dishes with a Persian and Middle Eastern accent. Warm, generous and delicious.]]

[[pick: Alegria | A fully vegan restaurant in the north of the city. Worth the trip if you're staying nearby.]]

[[pick: The Green Roll: Vegan Sushi Bar | Creative, fully vegan sushi on Ahad Ha'am Street. Proof that sushi doesn't need fish.]]

## Vegan-friendly favourites

[[pick: Meshek Barzilay | A vegetarian restaurant in Neve Tzedek with a farm-to-table approach and plenty of vegan dishes, including a weekend brunch that's a local favourite.]]

[[pick: Almaz Mendel | An Ethiopian restaurant in Florentin, and kosher too. Ethiopian food is a vegan's dream: lentil and vegetable stews on injera bread, eaten with your hands.]]

[[pick: Market Vegan | A restaurant on Dizengoff with dishes from around the world, from Indian and Thai to Moroccan and Persian, with lots of vegan options.]]

[[pick: Flatbush | A Caribbean kitchen on Yehuda HaLevi Street with tables outside and plenty of vegan dishes. Something different, and very good.]]

[[pick: Carnal | Mexican food in Florentin, open every day from noon to midnight, with vegan tacos and tables outside.]]

## Vegan street food

Much of Tel Aviv's street food is vegan by nature:

- **Falafel** in pita with salad and tahini.
- **Hummus, msabbaha and ful:** chickpeas, tahini, lemon, garlic and olive oil. See [the best hummus](/magazine/best-hummus-tel-aviv).
- **Sabich without egg.**
- **Majadra:** rice or bulgur with lentils and caramelised onions.

## FAQ

### Is Tel Aviv really the vegan capital of the world?

It's often called that, and it's easy to see why: a high share of vegans and vegetarians, dozens of fully vegan restaurants, and vegan options on almost every menu.

### What are the best fully vegan restaurants in Tel Aviv?

Anastasia, Goodness, BANA and Zakaim are some of the best, plus The Green Roll for vegan sushi.

### Is Israeli food vegan-friendly?

Very. Hummus, falafel, tahini, salads and many traditional dishes are naturally vegan. Just watch for eggs in sabich and dairy in some baked goods.

### How do I ask for vegan food in Hebrew?

Say "ani tivoni" (I'm vegan, male) or "ani tivonit" (female). Most people in Tel Aviv also speak English and understand "vegan".

### Is kosher food vegan?

Not necessarily, but kosher dairy restaurants have no meat, and kosher meat restaurants have no dairy, which can make vegan options easier to spot. Look for "parve", which means no meat and no dairy.
