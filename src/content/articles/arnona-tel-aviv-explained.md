Arnona (ארנונה) is the municipal property tax, and in Tel Aviv-Yafo it's one of the biggest monthly costs after rent. It pays for rubbish collection, street cleaning, parks, schools and the rest of the city's services. Here's how it works and how to make sure you're not paying more than you should.

## Who pays

The person living in the property pays, not necessarily the owner. If you rent, your lease almost always makes you responsible for arnona, and you register with the city in your own name from the first day of the lease. When you move out, tell the city the date, and the account moves to the next tenant or back to the owner.

If you forget to register, bills keep going to the previous occupant or the owner, and you may end up paying arrears with interest later. Register in the first week.

## How it's calculated

Arnona isn't based on what the flat is worth. It's based on:

1. **Size.** The billable area in square metres, as measured by the city. Balconies and storage rooms can count, depending on the rules.
2. **Use.** Homes pay a residential rate; offices and shops pay much more.
3. **Zone and building type.** The city divides itself into zones with different rates per square metre.

So two flats of the same size on the same street usually pay roughly the same, whatever the rent. If your bill seems high, check the area the city has on file against the real size of the flat. Mistakes happen, and you can ask for a re-measurement.

## How to pay

Bills come every two months. You can pay each bill, set up a standing order from your bank account or card, or pay for the whole year upfront. The city's website lists the current options and any early-payment discount. Set up the standing order on day one and you'll never get a late-payment letter.

## Discounts: check if you qualify

Arnona discounts are set by national regulations and each city's own decisions. The common ones:

- **New immigrants:** up to 90% off for up to 100 square metres of your home, for 12 months within your first 24 months in Israel. You have to apply: bring your ID, your oleh certificate and the lease to the city's arnona department, or apply online. If you lived in another city first, bring a letter saying you didn't use the discount there.
- **Pensioners:** discounts for people of retirement age, depending on income and pensions.
- **People with disabilities** and their families, depending on the disability percentage and allowances.
- **Low income:** discounts based on household income.
- **Soldiers and reservists:** discounts for lone soldiers and for reservists during long call-ups, which have expanded since the war.
- **Empty property:** a reduced rate for a short period when a property is genuinely empty, under the city's rules.

Discounts aren't automatic. You apply, usually once a year, and they're rarely given retroactively for more than a short period. The Tel Aviv-Yafo municipality website has the forms and current rules, and Kol Zchut explains each discount in plain Hebrew, English and other languages.

## Disputes and appeals

If you think the area, the classification or the person billed is wrong, you can file an objection (השגה) with the arnona manager, usually within 90 days of getting the bill. If it's rejected, there's an appeals committee. Keep paying in the meantime to avoid interest.

## FAQ

### Do tenants pay arnona in Tel Aviv?

Usually yes. The lease almost always puts arnona on the tenant, who registers with the city in their own name for the length of the lease.

### How much is the new immigrant arnona discount?

Up to 90% off for up to 100 square metres of your home, for 12 months within the first 24 months after aliyah. You have to apply for it at the city's arnona department.

### Can I get money back if I was overcharged?

Sometimes. File an objection with the arnona department as soon as you notice, with the lease and a measurement of the flat. Retroactive refunds are limited, so don't wait.
