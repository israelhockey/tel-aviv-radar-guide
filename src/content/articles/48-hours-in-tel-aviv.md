Two days is enough to fall for Tel Aviv. This plan mixes the classics with the things locals actually do, and links to live pages so you can check what's open as you go.

## Day one

**Morning: breakfast and the market.** Start with shakshuka or an Israeli breakfast (see [the best breakfast in town](/magazine/best-breakfast-tel-aviv)), then wander the **Carmel Market** while it's still calm. If it's Tuesday or Friday, the craft fair on **Nahalat Binyamin** is right next door.

[[photo: city:carmel | The Carmel Market]]

**Afternoon: the beach.** Walk west to the sea. **Gordon** and **Frishman** are central and lively. Check today's [beach score and best hours](/beach) first.

**Evening: Jaffa at sunset.** Walk south along the promenade to **Old Jaffa** for the sunset over the port, then dinner in Jaffa.

**Night: out.** Tel Aviv nights start late. See [tonight's events](/events) and [the best cocktail bars](/magazine/best-cocktail-bars-tel-aviv).

## Day two

**Morning: the White City.** Coffee on Rothschild Boulevard, then walk among the Bauhaus buildings that earned Tel Aviv its UNESCO listing.

[[photo: city:whitecity | Rothschild Boulevard]]

**Midday: Neve Tzedek.** The city's oldest neighbourhood, with galleries, boutiques and quiet lanes. HaTachana, the restored old train station, is a few minutes away.

**Afternoon: the port or Yarkon Park.** Rent a Tel-O-Fun bike and ride the coast north to the port, or into Yarkon Park.

**Evening: street food and a show.** Grab a pita from [our street food picks](/magazine/best-street-food-tel-aviv), then catch a show.
