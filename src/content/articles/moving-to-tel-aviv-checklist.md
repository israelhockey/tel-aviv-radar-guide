Welcome. Moving to Tel Aviv is exciting and, for the first few weeks, a little overwhelming. Here's a checklist to get the essentials done, roughly in the order you'll need them.

## Week one

1. **Get a Rav-Kav.** The rechargeable transport card works on buses, trains and the light rail. You can also pay with a ticketing app such as Moovit or Rav-Kav Online. See [Getting around](/getting-around).
2. **Get a local SIM.** Israel's mobile plans are cheap. Bring your passport.
3. **Learn the week.** The weekend is Friday and Saturday, and on Shabbat most public transport stops. Our [Shabbat page](/shabbat) shows candle-lighting times and what still runs.

## Week two

4. **Health fund (kupat holim).** If you're a new citizen or resident, you'll register with one of the four health funds. Ask friends which clinic is closest and has English-speaking staff.
5. **Bank account.** Bring your ID or passport, proof of address and patience. Appointments help.
6. **Arnona.** Municipal property tax is billed by the city to whoever lives in the apartment. Register as soon as you move in, and ask about discounts if you're a new immigrant or student.

## Week three and four

7. **Join DigiTel**, the city's free resident club, for discounts on culture and city updates.
8. **Start Hebrew.** Even a little goes a long way. See our guide to [learning Hebrew in Tel Aviv](/magazine/learn-hebrew-tel-aviv).
9. **Find your people.** Meetups, language exchanges, run clubs and beach volleyball. See [meeting people in Tel Aviv](/magazine/meet-people-tel-aviv).

## Useful numbers

- **106**: the city hotline for almost anything municipal.
- **100** police, **101** ambulance (Magen David Adom), **102** fire.

[[tip: Keep digital copies of your passport, visa or ID, and rental contract. You'll be asked for them more often than you expect.]]
