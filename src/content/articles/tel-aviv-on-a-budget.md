Tel Aviv regularly ranks among the most expensive cities in the world. But some of its best things cost little or nothing. Here's how to enjoy it without spending a fortune.

## The beach is free

All the city's beaches are free to use. Sun loungers and umbrellas cost money if you rent them, but a towel on the sand doesn't. The promenade from the Port to Jaffa is the best free walk in town. Check today's [beach conditions](/beach).

## Eat like a local

The city's cheapest food is also some of its best: hummus, falafel, sabich and shawarma. Our guides to [the best hummus](/magazine/best-hummus-tel-aviv) and [the best street food](/magazine/best-street-food-tel-aviv) are full of places where lunch costs a fraction of a restaurant meal.

## Lunch deals and happy hours

- **Business lunch ("iskit"):** many restaurants offer a set lunch menu on weekdays, often half the dinner price.
- **Happy hour:** lots of bars have deals in the early evening. Our [Food page](/food) can filter for places with a happy hour.

## Get around for less

- **Walk.** The centre is compact, and most of it is flat.
- **Tel-O-Fun bikes** from stations around the city. See live bikes on the [live map](/live).
- **The light rail and buses** are cheap with a Rav-Kav card or a payment app.
- **Weekend shuttle:** on Shabbat, the city runs free "Naim BaSofash" shuttle lines. Routes on the [Shabbat page](/shabbat).

## Free things to do

Galleries, festivals, free concerts, markets and parks. Start with [free things to do in Tel Aviv](/magazine/free-things-to-do-tel-aviv) and this week's [free events](/free).

## Shop smart

- Buy groceries at the markets late in the day, when prices drop.
- Supermarkets are cheaper than corner shops ("makolet"), which charge for convenience.
- Bring a refillable bottle: the tap water is safe to drink.

## Sleep for less

Florentin and the city centre have good hostels with private rooms too. See [where to stay](/magazine/where-to-stay-in-tel-aviv) and our [hotels map](/hotels).
