Israel's craft beer scene is young, but it's grown up fast. Twenty years ago the choice was between two or three local lagers. Today there are dozens of small breweries around the country, from the Golan Heights to the Negev, making pale ales, stouts, sours and wheat beers, often with local twists like date honey, citrus or spices. Tel Aviv is where you can try most of them.

Pubs here range from proper craft beer bars with rotating taps to cosy neighbourhood places where everyone knows the bartender, plus a handful of English and Irish pubs for the football. Happy hour, usually in the early evening, is a Tel Aviv institution: look out for deals on pints.

## The picks

[[pick: Beer Bazaar | The best place to start exploring Israeli craft beer, with a long list of local brews on tap and in bottles and branches around the city. Staff are happy to give you a taste before you choose. Great for a beer flight.]]

[[pick: Dancing Camel | One of the pioneers of Israeli craft brewing, with its own brewpub on HaTaasiya Street in an industrial corner of the city. Drink the beer where it's made, with the brewing kit in sight.]]

[[pick: Porter & Sons | A beer bar and restaurant with a big tap list and a serious food menu to go with it. A good choice for a group that wants dinner and beer in the same place.]]

[[pick: Beer Garden | A beer garden in the Sarona complex, with tables outside among the restored Templer buildings. Ideal for an after-work pint on a warm evening.]]

[[pick: Mike's Place | A Tel Aviv institution on the beachfront on Herbert Samuel Street, open from morning until four in the morning. Live music, sport on the screens, a big international crowd and beer by the pitcher. If there's a big game on, this is where to watch it.]]

[[pick: Molly Bloom's | An Irish pub with outdoor seating, Guinness on tap and a friendly crowd of locals and expats. Good for a pint and a match.]]

[[pick: Shtrasse | A neighbourhood beer bar on Ibn Gvirol, a short walk from Rabin Square. Unfussy, local and a good place to try a few beers.]]

[[pick: Alcohome | A craft beer shop and bar on Ben Yehuda Street in the Old North, where you can drink in or take bottles home. A great place to find small Israeli breweries.]]

[[pick: Herzl 16 | A bar and restaurant at Herzl 16, near Neve Tzedek, open from morning until midnight. A relaxed spot for a beer with food at any time of day.]]

[[pick: Uganda | A bar with tables outside, open until two every night and from midday at the weekend. Good music, a cool crowd and cold beer.]]

## Israeli beers to look for

- **Local craft brewers:** you'll find beers from small breweries all over Israel on the taps in this guide. Ask for something local.
- **Goldstar:** Israel's classic lager, cheap and cold. You'll see it everywhere.
- **Seasonal specials:** many breweries make beers with dates, citrus, pomegranate or spices.
- **Arak and grapefruit:** not a beer, but the classic Tel Aviv drink to try at least once.

## FAQ

### What is the best craft beer bar in Tel Aviv?

Beer Bazaar is the easiest way to try a wide range of Israeli craft beer. Dancing Camel lets you drink at the brewery itself, and Porter & Sons has one of the biggest tap lists in town.

### Where can I watch football or sport in Tel Aviv?

Mike's Place on the beachfront is the classic sports bar, open late with screens everywhere. Irish pubs like Molly Bloom's also show big matches.

### Is there a brewery I can visit in Tel Aviv?

Dancing Camel brews on site at its brewpub on HaTaasiya Street, so you can drink the beer where it's made.

### What's the local beer in Israel?

Goldstar is the classic Israeli lager, and Maccabi is another common one. For something more interesting, ask any craft bar for a local pale ale or seasonal beer.
